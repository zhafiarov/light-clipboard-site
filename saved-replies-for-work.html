Saved replies for support, sales and recruiting on iPhone

A Light Clipboard guide · 20 October 2026

Most work messages sent from a phone are versions of the same few: where is my order, it is late, I am away, come for an interview, just checking in, please pay. Here are six templates to copy and adapt, rules for writing ones that still sound like you, and how to keep them one tap away on an iPhone.

Six templates to copy

Square brackets mark what changes each time; keep them, since a visible [Name] is easier to spot than a blank.

Order status

Hi [Name], thanks for checking in. Your order [number] left us on [date] with [carrier]; you can follow it here: [tracking link]. It should arrive by [date]. If it hasn't by then, reply here and I'll sort it out.
[Your name], [Company]

Shipping delay

Hi [Name], I'm sorry: order [number] is running late. [One line on why.] The new estimate is [date]. You don't need to do anything; I'll message you the moment it ships. If you'd rather cancel for a full refund, reply "cancel" and I'll do it today.
[Your name], [Company]

Out of office

Thanks for your message. I'm away until [date] and checking messages only now and then. For anything urgent, contact [colleague] at [email or phone]. Everything else I'll answer when I'm back.
[Your name]

Interview invitation

Hi [Name], thank you for applying for the [role] position at [Company]. We'd like to invite you to a [30-minute] [video / phone] interview with [interviewer], our [title]. Would [day, time] or [day, time] suit you? Reply with the one that works and I'll send an invitation.
[Your name], [Company]

Follow-up

Hi [Name], following up on my message from [day] about [topic]. If the timing is wrong, no problem; tell me when to check back. If it's right, I can [send the proposal / set up a call] this week. Which would you prefer?
[Your name]

Invoice reminder

Hi [Name], a quick reminder that invoice [number] for [amount] was due on [due date]. You can pay by [method]: [details]. If it's already on its way, thank you and please ignore this. If anything is unclear, reply and I'll help.
[Your name], [Company]

Writing templates that still sound like you

  • Lead with the answer. The first line settles the question; the rest comes after.
  • Say what happens next, and when. "I'll message you when it ships" spares the customer a second question.
  • Keep the placeholders few. A saved reply is typed as it is and you fill the gaps afterwards; two or three brackets take ten seconds, ten make the template slower than typing.
  • Make a short version for chat. Email carries a greeting and a sign-off; in WhatsApp or Messages the same reply is better without them.
  • Name templates by the question, not the answer: Order status, Late, Interview, Pay. You will be looking for them in a hurry.

Keeping them one tap away

A template only saves time if sending it is faster than typing it. On an iPhone there are three places to keep one: Text Replacement in Settings, for one-liners with a trigger you will remember; the quick replies inside WhatsApp Business or Instagram, if every conversation happens there; and a snippet keyboard, which shows your templates as keys in any app. Our canned responses guide compares them.

With Light Clipboard, you save each template in the app under the name you chose above, and it appears as a tile on the keyboard. In any app, hold the globe key, switch to Light Clipboard, tap the tile, switch back and fill in the brackets. The keyboard never asks for Full Access: it has no network connection and reads only the snippets you saved; our text expander guide explains the switch, and the technical article shows how the keyboard is built. Templates stay on your device; there is no sync, so an iPad keeps its own list.

To add the keyboard, open Settings, then General, then Keyboard, then Keyboards, tap Add New Keyboard and choose Light Clipboard. If you build a template in Shortcuts, the Add Snippet action saves it without opening the app.

Questions people ask

How many saved replies do I need?

Start with the three questions you answered most last week, and add one each time you catch yourself retyping something. A short list you know by name beats a long one you scroll through.

Will customers notice that I use templates?

Only if a template goes out unchanged. Fill in the brackets, add one line that applies to this person alone, and keep your usual tone; the template carries the structure so your attention goes to that line.

Can I use the same templates for email and chat?

Yes, but keep two versions of the ones you send most: a full one with greeting and sign-off for email, and a shorter one for chat, where the thread already carries the context.

Where should I keep templates so they work in every app on iPhone?

Text Replacement in Settings works in any app and suits short phrases. A snippet keyboard such as Light Clipboard works in any app and suits whole replies. Quick replies inside WhatsApp Business or Instagram work only there.
